# Stereo camera mount v0.1

A desk-standing test fixture that holds the two Arducam B0332 cameras below a monitor. It
gives you a readable, lockable baseline (70–160 mm) and independent yaw and pitch (±25°)
for each camera. Everything is printed in PETG and held together with one bolt size,
M4 × 16. Every adjustment locks with a printed thumb knob, so no tools are needed.

Geometry, STLs, the `.blend` file and the renders are generated by
[`hardware/mount-v0.1`](../../hardware/mount-v0.1/README.md). The camera dimensions it
relies on, and which of them are assumed, are in [camera-b0332.md](camera-b0332.md).

![Assembly seen from the user's side](../../hardware/mount-v0.1/renders/assembly_iso.png)

## Coordinates and settings

These are the same conventions the diagnostics software uses, and they are named
parameters in the generator:

- **Rig origin:** the midpoint between the two optical centres. **+X** points to the user's
  right, **+Y** up, and **+Z** toward the user. `cam_left` is at −X and `cam_right` at +X,
  as seen by the user facing the cameras.
- **Baseline:** the distance between the two optical axes at the pivot plane.
- **Angles:** `yaw_deg` and `pitch_deg` are 0 when the camera looks along +Z. Positive yaw
  turns the camera toward the rig centre, and positive pitch turns it up.
- **Pivots:** the yaw and pitch axes both pass through the assumed optical centre, which
  is on the lens axis 12 mm in front of the PCB. Changing the angles therefore does not
  move the optical centre, and the baseline reading holds at any yaw or pitch.
- **Height:** the optical axes are 69 mm above the desk.

| `carriage_left` / `carriage_right` | 1 + 1 | Slides on the rail, guided by a 56 mm key in the slot, and is locked by two knobs. Carries the yaw scale and a V notch that reads the baseline. Engraved L/R |

| M2 × 10 screw, pan or cheese head | 8 | PCB to standoffs, from the lens side |
| M2 hex nut | 8 | Pressed into the pockets on the back of the holder |
| Cable tie, ≤ 3.6 mm wide | 6 | Strain relief at the holder and at the foot |
| Self-adhesive rubber bumper, Ø 10–12 mm | 4 | Under the foot pads |
| PETG filament | ≈ 150–200 g | Estimate from the part volumes (295 cm³ solid) at the infill below. Your slicer gives the real figure |

No washers are needed. The knob pockets are shallower than the head and nut, so the steel
bears on the plastic and the knob only turns it. The joint stack-ups, with full nut
engagement and room for each bolt tip, are checked by the generator.

## Printing (PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle)

No part needs supports. Each STL is exported lying in its print orientation, centred, on z = 0.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Walls | 4 for the yokes, knobs and holders; 3 for everything else |
| Top / bottom layers | 5 / 4 |
| Infill | 30 % gyroid for the yokes, holders and knobs; 20 % for the base and carriages |
| Temperatures | Nozzle 235–245 °C, bed 75–85 °C (use the profile tuned for your filament) |
| Bed | 220 × 220 mm or larger. The base is 216 × 135 mm |
| Elephant-foot compensation | 0.15 mm. The yaw scale is engraved on the face of the carriage that prints on the bed |

| STL | Orientation (already applied) |
|---|---|
| `base` | Underside down. The tunnel and the cable channel print as short bridges |
| `carriage_*` | Upside down: the key points up and the scale face is on the bed |
| `yoke_*` | Base down. The pitch hole is a teardrop |
| `camera_holder` | Rear plate down. The fins have 45° undersides, the pitch hole is a teardrop and the nut pockets print flat side up |
| `knob` | Hex pocket up |

## First print: the fit coupons (about 1 hour)

Print `coupon_pcb`, `coupon_rail`, `coupon_slider` and one `knob` together, then check:

1. **PCB on `coupon_pcb`:** the camera sits flat on the four standoffs and M2 × 10 screws
   pass through the PCB holes into M2 nuts pressed into the back pockets. If your board has
   no holes on the 34 mm square, try the 28 mm through-holes. Then report which pattern
   exists, because the holder only has standoffs on the 34 mm square.
2. **Rear clearance:** nothing on the back of the PCB touches the plate. Standoffs are 5 mm,
   and the window is 26 × 26 mm.
3. **Pitch joint on `coupon_pcb`:** an M4 nut presses into the side pocket, and a bolt goes
   through the teardrop hole and threads into it.
4. **Rail on `coupon_rail`:** an M4 nut slides through the tunnel without spinning.
   `coupon_slider` drops into the slot and slides with light friction. Bolt the slider down
   with a knob and an M4 × 16 into the tunnel nut: it must clamp firmly, and the bolt tip
   must not stick out of the bottom of the tunnel.
5. **Knob:** the knob presses onto an M4 hex head and onto a nut, and does not slip when
   tightened by hand.

If a hole or pocket is too tight or loose, change the matching value in `Fasteners` or
`Print` in `params.py` (the values marked FIT) and rebuild.

## Assembly

1. **Rail nuts:** slide two M4 nuts into each end of the rail tunnel.
2. **Carriages:** set each carriage on the rail with its key in the slot, L on the user's
   left. Press an M4 × 16 head into each of two knobs. Put the bolts through the carriage
   into the tunnel nuts and snug them.
3. **Yokes:** drop an M4 × 16 into the hex pocket on top of the yoke base, so the head sits
   flush. Set the yoke on the carriage with its arm on the outer side. Under the carriage,
   press a nut into a knob and thread it onto the bolt.
4. **Cameras:** press M2 nuts into the back of the holder, then screw the PCB on from the lens
   side with the connector at the window. Press an M4 nut into the inner pocket of the
   holder wall that will face the arm.
5. **Holders:** hold each holder in its yoke, then put a knob with an M4 × 16 through the arm
   into the holder nut.
6. **Cables:** plug in the USB cable. Tie it to the slot pair on the rear plate, leaving a
   loose loop for yaw and pitch. Run it down behind the carriage and along the channel under
   the rail, then tie it at the slot pair on the foot.
7. **Feet:** stick the rubber bumpers under the foot pads.

## Setting and reading the adjustments

| Adjustment | Unlock | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | the two knobs on the carriage | The V notch in the carriage's back edge against the rail scale (read from behind). With both carriages at the same mark, the mark *is* the baseline. Ticks every 5 mm, numbers every 20 mm, long tick at 100 |
| Yaw | the knob under the carriage | The pointer on the yoke against the carriage ticks: every 5°, long every 10°. The dimple marks the **positive** side, toward the rig centre |
| Pitch | the knob on the yoke arm | The front face of the holder's fin against the ticks on the arm rim. The dimple marks **positive**, up |

Hold the camera while you tighten the yaw or pitch knob, because friction can drag it
along with the knob. Record the settings as
`baseline, cam_left yaw/pitch, cam_right yaw/pitch`, in the units above.

## What has been verified, and how

**CAD-level checks.** These ran in the cloud on Blender 5.2.3 by `build.py`, and the
results are in [`validation/report.json`](../../hardware/mount-v0.1/validation/report.json):

- **Meshes:** all 10 printable meshes are closed and manifold, each is a single body
  with positive volume, and each fits a 220 × 220 mm bed.
- **Overhangs:** the only faces steeper than 45° are about 22 mm² on the base (inside the
  engraved numbers) and 0.6 mm² on the right carriage (the "R"). Everything else is a
  bridge over a hole or channel.
- **Camera fit:** the reference camera, with its assumed dimensions, does not overlap the
  holder. The PCB is seated on the standoffs with 1 mm side clearance. The lens is
  ≥ 8.5 mm from the holder, and the assumed rear parts are ≥ 0.5 mm from it. The connector
  is 7 mm from the holder, and the plug-and-cable keep-out is 8 mm from it.
- **Motion:** 195 poses gave no collisions between any parts, cameras included. The sweep
  covers baselines 70, 100 and 160 mm, yaw −25…+25° in 5° steps, pitch −25…+25° in 12.5°
  steps, and both cameras turned the same and opposite ways. A self-test confirms the
  checker reports a deliberate overlap.
- **Connector access:** the USB plug and cable keep-out does not hit any other part in
  any pose.
- **Field of view:** the view stays clear except when pitched down 25°. There the base
  enters the bottom of the image, and at baseline 70 with +20° yaw so does the other
  carriage. Looking down is not the intended use below a monitor.
- **Fasteners:** every M4 joint fully engages its nut (3.2 mm). The bolt tip has 0.5 mm of
  room in the rail tunnel, 5.6 mm of knob clearance above the foot, and 11.8 mm beside the
  lens.

**Not verified.** These need the physical cameras and a printer:

- Every camera dimension marked ASSUMED, in particular the hole pattern, the rear
  component height, the connector position and the optical centre.
- Printed tolerances: the press fits, the slide fit of the key, and the teardrop holes.
- Holding torque of the knob locks, repeatability of the settings, and the accuracy of
  the scales.
- Stiffness and vibration on a real desk, and tipping when a cable is pulled.
- Whether a 69 mm axis height suits your monitor's bezel.

## Physical tests to run

1. **The coupons above.** Report the hole pattern, the fit of the pockets and the
   rail/slider clamp.
2. **Measurements** listed in [camera-b0332.md](camera-b0332.md). Send them to the thread,
   and the generator will be rebuilt with the measured values.
3. **Baseline scale:** set 70, 100 and 160 mm. Measure between the lens barrel centres with
   calipers; the error should be under 1 mm.
4. **Angles:** set pitch to 0 and ±20° and check with a phone inclinometer held on the
   holder back. Do the same for yaw against a printed protractor.
5. **Locks:** tighten by hand, then push the lens sideways with one finger. Nothing should
   move.
6. **Repeatability:** set, disturb and reset an angle five times, recording the camera
   image each time. The diagnostics software can then report the image shift.
7. **Stability:** tap the desk and type next to the rig, and watch for image shake in the
   live preview.

## Known limitations of v0.1

- **Holder hole pattern:** the holder has standoffs only on the 34 mm hole square, so a
  board with only the 28 mm set needs one parameter changed.
- **Scale access:** the baseline scale is read from behind the rig.
- **Single arm:** the pitch arm is on one side only, chosen to allow the 70 mm minimum
  baseline. It is stiff enough on paper, but untested.
- **Height:** there is no height adjustment. Raise the rig on a block if 69 mm is too
  low for your monitor.
